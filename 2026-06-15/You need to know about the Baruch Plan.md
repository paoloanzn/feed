### Main Topic: Nuclear Control

**Main Thesis:** International agreements on dangerous technology are extremely difficult to achieve, and partial, targeted enforcement is more effective than trying to establish a single global monopoly.

**Simple Summary:** The article uses the history of the nuclear arms race, specifically the failed Baruch Plan, to teach lessons about managing powerful, world-changing technologies like the new AI. It shows that even when world leaders agree on a plan to control dangerous weapons, political rivalries prevent full cooperation. The failure of the nuclear agreement demonstrates that trying to create one single global authority is less effective than setting up smaller, focused international controls.

**The Bottom Line:** > When facing massive technological risks, focus on targeted international cooperation rather than seeking a single, all-powerful global authority, as achieving true peace requires pragmatic, partial measures.
