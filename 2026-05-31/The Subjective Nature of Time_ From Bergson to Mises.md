### Main Topic: Time and Economics

**Main Thesis:** Mainstream economics fails because it treats time as a fixed, measurable quantity instead of recognizing that time is a subjective, lived experience.

**Simple Summary:** The article argues that there is a crucial difference between "clock time" (objective, measurable time) and "duration" (the time we actually experience). Philosophers like Henri Bergson showed that our personal experience of time is fluid and qualitative, not fixed. Because human actions and economic choices are based on this subjective experience, trying to measure time mathematically in economic models is fundamentally flawed. This distinction shows that time is a unique, non-transferable resource that cannot be captured by standard mathematical formulas.

**The Bottom Line:** > Economic models fail to predict human behavior because they ignore the subjective reality of time. The true value of time is known only by the individual acting, not by any external equation.
