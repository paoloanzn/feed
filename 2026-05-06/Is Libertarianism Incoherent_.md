### Main Topic: Political philosophy

**Main Thesis:** American libertarianism lacks a fixed core because it is a shifting collection of different, fragmented groups rather than a single, stable system of ideas.

**Simple Summary:** Philosopher Matt Zwolinski argues that libertarianism is not a single, cohesive philosophy but a collection of shifting groups or "tribes." He points out that the supposed consensus on libertarianism has always been temporary, and different factions (like those focused on social justice versus pure free markets) operate under the same label. To solve political problems, people must accept that different principles have different meanings and that there is no single, absolute answer, requiring practical wisdom instead of rigid, single-rule systems.

**The Bottom Line:** > To achieve practical solutions, people must stop demanding a single, rigid set of rules and instead use flexible, practical wisdom to balance competing values, recognizing that different political groups have fundamentally different goals.
