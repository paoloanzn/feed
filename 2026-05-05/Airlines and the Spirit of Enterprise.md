### Main Topic: Airline Competition

**Main Thesis:** Mergers in the airline industry are generally fine if they lower costs, and the survival of the industry depends more on profit than on the exact number of competitors.

**Simple Summary:** The article discusses a failed attempt to merge two airlines, JetBlue and Spirit, which was blocked by the government because it would create too much competition. The author argues that simply counting the number of rivals is not the right way to judge competition. Instead, the history of the airline industry shows that cost-cutting and survival depend on making money, not just having many competitors. The author concludes that the best way to guarantee jobs in the industry is for the successful companies to make profits, not for the struggling ones to receive government subsidies.

**The Bottom Line:** > The focus should be on making money and cutting costs, as this is what determines which airlines survive, rather than focusing on whether there are exactly four or five companies in the market.
