### Main Topic: Money and Rules

**Main Thesis:** The attempt to govern money by simple rules ignores the complex, contextual knowledge that money embodies, suggesting that monetary order is something that emerges rather than is constructed.

**Simple Summary:** The article discusses the shift in how money is controlled, moving from subjective judgment (Keynes) to fixed rules (Friedman). While rules make policy predictable, the author argues that money is not just a technical system that can be managed by simple rules. Money reflects complex human knowledge, which is too fragmented and contextual to be captured by simple rules. Therefore, trying to impose rules on money only organizes existing ignorance, and the ultimate question is whether monetary order can be created or if it must simply emerge from human action.

**The Bottom Line:** > Monetary control is not just a technical problem; it is a philosophical one, and simple rules cannot replace the deep, contextual knowledge that money represents.
