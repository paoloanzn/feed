### Main Topic: Moral rules and action

**Main Thesis:** Trying to follow strict moral rules (like "don't kill") is very difficult when trying to achieve good results, and finding the exact limits of these rules is often impossible in complex situations.

**Simple Summary:** The article explores how strict moral rules (called "deontological bars") interact with the goal of doing good (consequentialism). The author uses examples like assassinations, AI safety debates, and political activism to show that defining what is morally right is extremely complicated. The author struggles to find a simple rule that works in all situations, concluding that moral rules often break down or require complex interpretation when facing real-world choices.

**The Bottom Line:** > Moral rules are not simple, and figuring out when and how to break them depends heavily on context, making it hard to apply strict rules consistently in complex modern debates.
