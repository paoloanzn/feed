### Main Topic: Systemic Change

**Main Thesis:** Achieving large-scale societal change is extremely difficult because perfect plans don't exist, so focus instead on persistent, iterative action rather than waiting for a single perfect solution.

**Simple Summary:** The article describes the frustrating journey of trying to fix big problems in society. It argues that noticing a problem is the first step, but realizing that perfect, stable solutions are impossible leads to frustration. Trying to create a perfect plan often fails because real-world actors are unpredictable. Therefore, instead of seeking one perfect strategy, the most effective approach is to use small, continuous steps (like a "gradient descent") and dedicate yourself to the cause, knowing that incremental improvements matter more than waiting for a flawless outcome.

**The Bottom Line:** > Stop searching for the "perfect plan" to fix the world, and instead focus on taking small, consistent actions toward your goals. Keep working on the cause, even if you don't get all the credit for the final result.
