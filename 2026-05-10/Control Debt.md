### Main Topic: AI Safety Engineering

**Main Thesis:** Safety protocols are only as effective as the real-world engineering practices and infrastructure surrounding the AI system, and ignoring these practices creates dangerous gaps in safety assurance.

**Simple Summary:** When building safety rules for AI, researchers often assume perfect conditions, but in real labs, engineers make shortcuts (like sharing memory or using long-lived access) to save time. This creates "control debt"—hidden weaknesses—that makes safety estimates unreliable. The article argues that the effectiveness of AI safety depends entirely on how well systems are monitored, isolated, and logged, not just the protocol itself.

**The Bottom Line:** > To ensure AI safety is real, teams must measure how real-world engineering choices affect safety, and fix these hidden flaws now before deployment.
