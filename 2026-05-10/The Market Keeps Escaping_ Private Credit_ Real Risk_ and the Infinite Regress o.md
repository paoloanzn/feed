### Main Topic: Private Credit Risk

**Main Thesis:** Financial regulations often push risky activities into unregulated areas, and the current instability in the private credit market is caused more by distorted interest rates than by a lack of oversight.

**Simple Summary:** Financial rules often create new markets, like the "shadow banking" industry, by pushing activity outside of regulated banks. Private lending grew rapidly because banks left the market, and this growth is based on lenders knowing their borrowers deeply. However, this growth is now fragile because low interest rates distorted the true cost of money, forcing investors into riskier deals. The article argues that fixing the interest rate signal is more important for stability than simply adding more rules.

**The Bottom Line:** > Financial stability is best achieved by correcting the distorted cost of money, not by adding more regulatory walls around the market.
