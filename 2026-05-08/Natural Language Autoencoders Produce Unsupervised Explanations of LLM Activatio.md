### Summary of the Article

The article discusses the use of **Explainability** in machine learning, specifically exploring how to understand the internal workings of large models. It focuses on the potential of these explanations to uncover hidden information, and then delves into the practical application of these explanations in auditing and understanding model behavior.

### Key Findings and Arguments

1.  **Auditing Models:** The authors demonstrate that explanations can be used to audit models to detect issues, suggesting they are valuable tools for safety and reliability.
2.  **Limitations of Explanations:** The explanations are not perfect representations of the true internal reasoning. They can reveal correlations, but they may miss the true causal mechanisms.
3.  **The Nature of Explanations:** The explanations are essentially mappings from complex internal states to human-understandable language, which means they are inherently interpretive.
4.  **The Challenge of Misrepresentation:** Explanations can be misleading, as they might focus on superficial correlations rather than the true cause.
5.  **Application to Misalignment:** The discussion touches on the challenge of aligning model behavior with human intentions, suggesting that relying solely on explanations might lead to a false sense of understanding.

### Conclusion

The article concludes that while explanations are a powerful tool for auditing, their utility lies in guiding further investigation rather than providing a complete, definitive answer. They are a necessary step in the pursuit of trustworthy AI, but they must be used cautiously, acknowledging their interpretive nature.
