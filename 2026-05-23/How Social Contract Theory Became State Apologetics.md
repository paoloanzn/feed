### Main Topic: Social Contract Theory

**Main Thesis:** Social contract theory, which was meant to limit government, ultimately provided the most powerful justification for the state's authority.

**Simple Summary:** The article explores how ideas from thinkers like Locke and Hobbes—known as social contract theory—were used to justify the formation of governments. Although these theories started as a way to limit power and establish consent, they ironically evolved to become a tool that justifies the modern state's power. This process turned the idea of "consent" into a reason why people must obey the government, making state actions seem voluntary rather than coercive.

**The Bottom Line:** > The history of social contract theory shows that the idea of government, rather than limiting power, ended up making individual rights dependent on the state, providing the state with a sophisticated excuse to expand its authority.
