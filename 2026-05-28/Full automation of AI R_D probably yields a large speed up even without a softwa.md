This text delves into the complex relationship between computational resources, human labor, and the rate of technological progress, particularly in the context of Artificial Intelligence and software development.

Here is a summary of the key arguments:

### Core Thesis

The central argument is that the efficiency gains derived from automation (like AI) significantly accelerate the rate of progress. The author uses economic modeling to suggest that the productivity of human effort is being multiplied by the ability of machines to handle complex tasks, leading to a much faster rate of innovation than traditional models predict.

### Key Takeaways

1.  **Productivity Multiplier:** The analysis suggests that the current pace of progress is not solely determined by human effort but is heavily influenced by the increased capability of automated systems. This efficiency gain acts as a multiplier on overall growth.
2.  **Impact of Automation:** The discussion explores how the ability of systems to automate tasks changes the dynamics of economic growth. When automation is factored in, the potential for progress increases substantially.
3.  **Growth Dynamics:** The text touches upon concepts related to growth models (like the Cobb-Douglas function) to quantify how changes in inputs (labor vs. capital/computation) affect the output (progress).
4.  **Implications for the Future:** The analysis implies that the current technological trajectory, driven by these efficiency gains, sets a new, potentially much higher benchmark for future progress.

In essence, the passage argues that **AI and automation are not just tools for doing existing work faster; they fundamentally change the rules of economic and technological growth.**
