### Main Topic: Cognitive Augmentation

**Main Thesis:** Augmenting human memory and working capacity might be achieved more efficiently by exploiting the temporal and frequency patterns of brain activity rather than simply increasing raw computational power.

**Simple Summary:** The author argues that growing AI to replace biological neurons is complex and risky. Instead, evolution suggests that the brain uses modular structures and timing (time coding) to organize information. By drawing an analogy to radio waves, the author suggests that multiple brain signals (frequencies) can combine to "bind" separate concepts into larger, abstract ideas. This method suggests that brainwave timing could encode control information, allowing the brain to manage memory more efficiently than current methods.

**The Bottom Line:** > To significantly boost working memory, we should focus on understanding how the timing and relationship between brain signals encode concepts, rather than just seeking more raw processing power.
