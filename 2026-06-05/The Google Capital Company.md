### Main Topic: Business Models

**Main Thesis:** The most profitable businesses are those that maximize relative value by minimizing costs and leveraging free supply, rather than focusing solely on the absolute amount of money they make.

**Summary:**
This article explores the dynamics of value creation in the modern economy, using the example of technology giants and the emerging field of AI infrastructure. It argues that success lies not just in maximizing revenue, but in understanding the flow of resources and the leverage provided by assets.

The text analyzes how companies like Google operate by leveraging vast information networks. It then pivots to the critical role of capital and infrastructure, particularly in the context of Artificial Intelligence. The author examines the tension between maximizing immediate profit and building long-term, scalable value.

A key theme is the relationship between capital, demand, and supply, illustrated by the complex interplay between companies seeking to fund massive AI projects (like those involving compute power) and the entities providing that infrastructure. The piece concludes by looking forward, suggesting that the future of value will be determined by who controls the essential resources—whether it is data, capital, or physical compute—and how effectively they can leverage those resources to drive innovation.
