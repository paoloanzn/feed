### Main Topic: City Intersection Design

**Main Thesis:** The author argues that complex road designs, like diagonal streets, create unnecessary difficulty, and removing this complexity would make sense, even if practical obstacles prevent it.

**Simple Summary:** The article discusses a specific intersection in Portland that is considered difficult for cyclists. The author suggests that the complexity of the road design—specifically a diagonal road cutting through the grid—is a major source of problems. While acknowledging that removing the road would be impractical due to costs and politics, the author uses this example to question why complex, outdated designs persist and suggests that city planners should look for better ways to create simpler, more functional layouts.

**The Bottom Line:** > Even when facing huge practical and political obstacles, we should constantly question complex systems to see if simpler, better solutions are possible.
