### Summary of the Argument

The text explores the relationship between mental states (like consciousness) and physical reality, focusing on whether mental states cause physical events. The author ultimately argues that while the intuitive idea of causation is appealing, it is not necessary for explaining the correlation between the mind and the body.

The core argument is that **correlation** is sufficient, not causation. The author suggests that the relationship between the mind and the body can be explained through **correlation** (dependence) rather than strict **causation**.

### Key Concepts Discussed:

1.  **Causation vs. Correlation:** The distinction between A causing B and A and B being correlated.
2.  **Sufficiency of Correlation:** The argument that correlation is sufficient to explain the relationship between mental states and physical states.
3.  **Epistemic Challenges:** The text addresses the difficulty of establishing true causal links, especially when dealing with subjective experience.
4.  **Philosophical Frameworks:** The discussion touches upon structuralist views (like structuralism) and the role of physical laws versus subjective experience.

### Main Conclusion:

The author concludes that the focus should shift from trying to establish whether mental states *cause* physical states to understanding how they are *correlated* with them. The complex philosophical debates about strict causation are less important than understanding the dependence between the mind and the body.

### In essence:

The text argues that the apparent link between mind and body is better understood as a **correlation** than a strict **causal link**.
